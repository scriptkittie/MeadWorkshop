# Sources

Mead Workshop is built on three books and a few public references. This page says what the app
takes from each, and what it deliberately leaves out.

## The books

| Short name | Book |
|---|---|
| **Piatz** | Steve Piatz, *The Complete Guide to Making Mead: The Ingredients, Equipment, Processes, and Recipes for Crafting Honey Wine*. Voyageur Press, 2014. ISBN 978-0-7603-4564-1. |
| **Schramm** | Ken Schramm, *The Compleat Meadmaker: Home Production of Honey Wine from Your First Batch to Award-Winning Fruit and Herb Variations*. Brewers Publications, 2003. ISBN 978-0-937381-80-9. |
| **Katz** | Sandor Ellix Katz, *The Art of Fermentation*. Chelsea Green Publishing, 2012. ISBN 978-1-60358-286-5. |

If you make mead, buy them. They explain far more than this app can.

### What the app uses from them

- **Facts, numbers and methods**, each credited to its book and chapter on screen. For example:
  - Piatz's yeast-limit table and his before-fruit gravity method (ch.9).
  - The nutrient schedule (ch.4–5).
  - Schramm's fruit amounts per 5 gallons (Table 8.1) and his sweetness ranges.
  - The 40-points-per-pound honey rule.
  - Spice amounts the books give (ginger, nutmeg, cloves, mint, lavender, rose, chiles, oak…).
- **Short quotations** of a sentence or less, in quotation marks and credited, where the author's own
  words say it best. Examples: Schramm on vanilla, "not obvious in its presence but notable in its
  absence"; Piatz on spices, "you can always add more, but it is hard to remove excessive spices."
- **A recipe index.** The 43 book recipes appear by name, style, batch size, book and chapter only, so
  you can find them in your copy.

### What is not included

- **No passages of book text.** Everything on screen is written for this app, apart from the short
  quotations above.
- **No book recipes** (ingredients, amounts or directions). See "Book pack" in the README if you own the
  books and want them in the app.
- **No yeast-table comments in the book's wording.** The yeast notes (from the strain table in Piatz
  ch.4, itself compiled from the makers' descriptions) are summarized in the app's own words. The app
  labels them "Piatz's notes, summarized". The tolerance and temperature figures are the makers'
  published specifications.
- No images, tables reproduced as laid out, or other material from the books.

A word-for-word check is part of how this project was made: every file was compared with the three
books, and any run of 8 or more consecutive words in common was rewritten unless it is a short,
credited quotation, a title, or a plain fact such as a number table.

| Spice starting points where the books give none | [Gotmead: spicing a mead](https://gotmead.com/community/threads/spicing-a-mead-question.19941/latest), [metheglin questions](https://gotmead.com/community/threads/metheglin-questions.19635/latest), [vanilla](https://gotmead.com/community/threads/adding-vanilla-beans.10343/latest), [oak](https://gotmead.com/community/threads/how-much-oak.17718/latest), [coriander](https://gotmead.com/community/threads/coriander.8977/latest), [orange zest](https://gotmead.com/community/threads/citrus-mead.8317/latest); [AHA forum: cardamom](https://forum.homebrewersassociation.org/t/cardamom/10846), [cacao nibs](https://forum.homebrewersassociation.org/t/another-cacoa-nibs-post/15955); [HomebrewTalk: juniper](https://www.homebrewtalk.com/threads/juniper-berries.96860/post-3987049), [coffee](https://www.homebrewtalk.com/threads/coffee-mead.84906/post-916953); [Practical Self Reliance: chamomile](https://practicalselfreliance.com/chamomile-wine-mead); [Patrick Mooney: hibiscus](https://patrickbrianmooney.nfshost.com/~patrick/blogs/XPB/mead/2018/04/08/105.html) |

On screen, every amount says where it came from: the book and chapter, "web:" for a homebrewers'
figure from the pages above, or "app:" where the app fills in a level between published figures. All
of them are starting points; taste and adjust.
